## 一句话概括

The privacy-preserving neural network (PPNN) utilizes cryptographic techniques such as Secure Multi-Party Computation (MPC) to enable collaborative model training while maintaining data confidentiality. However, PPNN does not address data content security, leaving it vulnerable to backdoor attacks where maliciously crafted data samples introduce triggers that manipulate model behavior. Existing backdoor defense mechanisms typically rely on computationally intensive gradient based optimization or require high computational precision, making them impractical in the MPC context. To address these challenges, we propose a novel backdoor defense mechanism for PPNN, termed CIPHERBD. Rather than relying on computationally intensive gradient-based optimization and model retraining, CIPHERBD reconstructs the backdoor trigger using private maximum operations and low-cost additions applied to a small subset of backdoor samples. To mitigate the reliance on high-precision computation in identifying backdoor samples through sample-level feature difference analysis, we propose a pixel-independent trigger approximation method. This technique aims to reconstruct an approximate trigger rather than the original one embedded by the adversary. For enhanced efficiency in confirming the presence of backdoors, we incorporate a feature-strength test that reduces the number of private inference queries required for detection. This optimization decreases computational complexity from quadratic to linear related to the number of classes, thereby improving scalability in multi class settings. Additionally, we optimize the core operation of our trigger approximation methodology by introducing a novel paradigm for secure maximum/minimum computations. This approach reduces communication outside security comparisons by approximately 46% compared to the traditional paradigm. We evaluate CIPHERBD using three distinct neural network architectures and three benchmark datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness against three prominent backdoor attack strategies.
