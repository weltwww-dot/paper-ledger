## 一句话概括

Geo-indistinguishability (GI), as a formal notion of location privacy, is implemented by perturbing the users' exact locations using random noise. For location-sensitive scenarios, e.g., localization and navigation, the introduction of random noise would decrease the data utility. In this paper, we investigate alternative randomization mechanisms that can provide higher data utility than the commonly used planar Laplacian mechanism while keeping the same privacy performance. Specifically, we investigate the usage of elliptical distributions to increase data utility. Firstly, we derive two sufficient and necessary conditions to determine whether a randomization mechanism satisfies GI. Using these conditions, we verify the feasibility of elliptical distributions for achieving GI and find that, except for the planar Laplacian, the Student's$t$distribution can also satisfy GI. Secondly, we adopt the Student's$t$distribution into the randomization mechanism to achieve GI and quantify its performance in terms of privacy level and data utility. To optimize the data utility and privacy level of Student's$t$distribution in practical applications, we formulate two optimization problems: one aims to maximize data utility under a given privacy level, and the other aims to maximize the privacy guarantee under the constraint of data utility. Extensive experiments have been conducted in two typical location-based applications, including fingerprint-based indoor localization and location-based advertising. Both theoretical analysis and experimental results evaluate the performance of the Student's$t$distribution and provide insight into the selection of randomization mechanism under different scenarios by comparing Student's$t$mechanism with the Laplacian one.
